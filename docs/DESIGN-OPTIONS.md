# SecondShow design selection

The user selected option 2 (Queue), which is implemented in the app. The original static selection concepts were visually checked for readable hierarchy, one primary action, sample labeling, venue scope, dates/prices and no live-success or sending claims. The first drafts were corrected to remove unrelated venues and an artist outside the sample catalog. The concepts are not included in this repository.

| Selection | Main interaction | Reference and tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 — Journey | Past show → next two shows → review draft. Chronological rows and a single review strip. | [Airbnb Trips](https://news.airbnb.com/airbnb-2025-summer-release/). Clear for a new operator; slower for bulk comparison. |
| 2 — Queue | Curate a two-show queue, swap candidates, inspect the invitation alongside it. | [Spotify playlist and queue controls](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-05-07/experience-a-new-dimension-of-music-discovery-with-more-controls-and-enhanced-tools/). Strong music context and fast substitutions; more visually dense than the other directions. |
| 3 — Invitation | Compose and approve the recipient invitation first, with audience/shows/message in progressive disclosure. | [Apple Invites](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/02/introducing-apple-invites-a-new-app-that-brings-people-together/). The audience experience is immediately understandable; detailed operations require an extra expansion. |

The references are official published app screenshots, downloaded and inspected before generation. Their interaction patterns informed the concepts; these are original SecondShow designs, not clones or claims of affiliation. The real venue workflow remains operator selection → human review → export without sending. Import, evidence, exclusions and outcomes remain available in later or disclosed surfaces rather than competing on the primary screen.
